Nova Station 13
Server Rules

These standards exist to protect a fair, welcoming, and engaging shift for everyone aboard. Play your character, respect the people behind the screens, and help create stories worth remembering.

1. Purpose, scope & responsibility

Nova Station 13 is a multiplayer roleplaying environment. Every participant is responsible for helping maintain an atmosphere where people can enjoy the game without being targeted, harassed, or deliberately deprived of a meaningful experience.

  • These rules apply to the game server, its rounds, and official community spaces where conduct affects Nova Station 13 or Circuit Verse Community.
  • Players are expected to understand the rules before participating. Not knowing a rule does not automatically excuse a violation.
  • Staff may consider intent, impact, context, prior conduct, and the surrounding circumstances when reviewing an incident.
  • Rules are interpreted in good faith. Do not search for loopholes or deliberately push wording to the limit.
Core principle: Your character may have conflicts with other characters. You must still treat the real people playing those characters with basic respect.

2. General conduct & player respect

2.1 Respectful behaviour

Harassment, targeted hostility, hate speech, discriminatory slurs, threats, doxxing, stalking, or repeated unwanted contact are not permitted. This applies in-character and out-of-character when the conduct targets or intimidates a real player.

2.2 Names, characters & identity

Choose character names and identities that fit the setting and are not designed to offend, impersonate real people, advertise, or disrupt the round. Do not use character creation to evade a prior restriction or impersonate another player or staff member.

2.3 Sexual and otherwise inappropriate content

Keep interactions appropriate for a general gaming community. Explicit sexual content, sexual harassment, non-consensual sexual roleplay, and sexual content involving minors are prohibited. Do not use the setting as an excuse for content that would be unacceptable in the community.

2.4 Disruptive behaviour

Do not intentionally flood chat, spam sounds or emotes, obstruct ordinary play with repetitive nonsense, or repeatedly derail scenes after being asked to stop. Humour is welcome when it fits the moment; persistent disruption is not.

2.5 Multiple accounts

Do not use alternate accounts to evade bans, bypass restrictions, manipulate votes or round outcomes, impersonate others, or gain an unfair advantage. If you have a legitimate account-access issue, contact staff before using another account.

3. Roleplay standards

3.1 Play a believable character

Make decisions based on what your character knows, sees, hears, and reasonably understands. Give your character motivations and limitations. You are encouraged to create memorable personalities, but not to use a character as a vehicle for constant disruption.

3.2 Stay in character

Use in-character (IC) channels for character speech and out-of-character (OOC) channels for player communication. Avoid OOC references, memes, or player arguments in IC dialogue. Use the designated OOC channels when you need to clarify a mechanic or communicate with players.

3.3 Respect character consequences

Allow events to matter. Do not ignore injuries, credible threats, arrest, orders, or other meaningful events simply because they inconvenience your preferred playstyle. You do not have to agree with every character, but you should respond in a way that creates play rather than shutting it down without reason.

3.4 Character continuity

Do not use knowledge from previous rounds to target, protect, or favour a character in a new round unless the game explicitly establishes that knowledge. Avoid using recurring character names or familiar player habits as a basis for suspicion or retaliation.

3.5 Roleplay over winning

Nova Station 13 is not a competition to achieve the highest kill count, collect the strongest equipment, or dominate every encounter. Prioritise interesting interactions and fair opportunities for others to respond. A technically successful action can still be poor conduct if it needlessly ruins the round for others.

4. Metagaming & improper information

Metagaming is using information your character could not reasonably have obtained in the current round. It gives you an unfair advantage and undermines investigation, secrecy, and roleplay.

Not permitted

  • Sharing live round information through Discord, private messages, voice calls, streams, or other external channels.
  • Using ghost, observer, deadchat, admin, or spectator information to influence your living character.
  • Acting on antagonist identities, objectives, locations, or plans learned outside your character's experience.
  • Using another player's screen, stream, or account to gain information your character does not possess.
  • Coordinating with friends outside the game to assist, protect, or target players during a round.
  • Using information from a previous round to pursue revenge or provide an advantage in the current round.

Permitted communication

Use in-game radios, PDAs, messages, and other intended game systems. General mechanical questions in approved OOC channels may be acceptable, but do not use those channels to reveal active-round information or coordinate in-character actions.

When unsure: If your character would not know it without information from another player, a stream, a ghost, or an external chat, do not act on it. Ask staff if you need clarification.

5. Powergaming, self-antagging & fair play

5.1 Powergaming

Do not routinely gather weapons, restricted equipment, tools, or access “just in case” when there is no credible in-character reason. Do not turn ordinary jobs into combat-ready roles or bypass the intended limits of your position to gain an advantage.

5.2 Role and equipment boundaries

Use equipment and access in ways that make sense for your job and the current situation. Do not raid departments, stockpile scarce supplies, or take over another department's responsibilities when qualified personnel are available and there is no urgent need.

5.3 Self-antagonising

Unless your role or the round's rules explicitly permit it, do not independently create major criminal or destructive situations simply to manufacture action. Examples include random murder, mass sabotage, unauthorised bomb-making, or deliberately provoking security with no meaningful character motive.

5.4 Mechanical exploitation

Do not abuse game mechanics, bugs, map quirks, or unintended interactions to become effectively invulnerable, bypass security, duplicate resources, or gain an unreasonable advantage. Report suspected exploits to staff rather than spreading or repeatedly testing them in live rounds.

6. Griefing, sabotage & destructive conduct

Griefing is deliberate conduct intended to ruin the round, damage the station experience, or prevent others from playing. It is not limited to killing.

  • Randomly attacking, killing, abducting, or incapacitating crew without a valid roleplay or antagonist justification.
  • Destroying, sabotaging, or disabling critical station systems without a legitimate job-related, emergency, or antagonist reason.
  • Creating indiscriminate hazards in high-traffic areas or setting traps intended to catch uninvolved players.
  • Stealing, hiding, or destroying essential equipment solely to prevent a department from functioning.
  • Repeatedly blocking doors, hallways, consoles, escape routes, or essential work areas to frustrate other players.
  • Deliberately wasting critical supplies or sabotaging medical, engineering, security, or command operations without justification.
  • Disconnecting, suiciding, or otherwise abandoning a role to avoid consequences or deny others a reasonable resolution.

Antagonist status may permit certain forms of sabotage or violence, but it does not provide unlimited permission to disregard all other rules. See the antagonist section.

7. Conflict, violence & escalation

7.1 Give situations room to develop

Where practical, use dialogue, warnings, negotiation, investigation, or other roleplay before escalating to violence. A minor insult, disagreement, prank, or inconvenience is not normally a reasonable basis for lethal force.

7.2 Proportionate response

Respond proportionately to the threat. Do not jump immediately to severe injury or murder when a less extreme response would reasonably resolve the situation. Consider whether the other character has an opportunity to understand and respond to what is happening.

7.3 Self-defence and defence of others

You may defend yourself or another person against an immediate credible threat. Once the threat has ended or the attacker is no longer capable of continuing, do not continue inflicting harm without a further valid reason.

7.4 Consent and boundaries

Do not force prolonged humiliating, disturbing, or personally targeted interactions on another player. If a scene crosses a player's boundary, take the concern seriously and use OOC communication or contact staff. In-character conflict is not a licence for out-of-character abuse.

7.5 Death, cloning and round participation

Do not use suicide, disconnecting, or other out-of-character options to evade capture, avoid an ongoing scene, or deny a reasonable outcome. Follow the server's available respawn, ghost, cryogenic, and character-return mechanics. Do not return to a scene using information your character could not know.

8. Jobs, departments & chain of command

8.1 Perform the role you selected

Make a reasonable effort to perform your assigned job. You are not expected to be an expert, especially as a new player, but you should not deliberately abandon your responsibilities to interfere with another department or pursue unrelated power.

8.2 Respect department responsibilities

Allow the department assigned to a task to handle it where reasonably possible. Assist during emergencies, when requested, or when qualified personnel are unavailable. Do not routinely take over another role's duties simply because you can use the relevant machinery or equipment.

8.3 Chain of command

Follow reasonable in-character instructions from your department leadership and command staff. If an order is unsafe, impossible, abusive, or clearly contrary to your role's obligations, use appropriate in-character channels to raise the issue. Do not turn every disagreement into deliberate insubordination or OOC hostility.

8.4 Command positions

Players selecting command roles are expected to provide direction, communicate clearly, delegate, and make a good-faith effort to keep the station functioning. Do not select a command position solely to access equipment, authority, or restricted areas while ignoring the responsibilities of the role.

9. Security, arrests & law enforcement roleplay

Security personnel are expected to protect the crew, investigate credible incidents, and use the authority of their role responsibly.

  • Have an in-character reason to detain, search, arrest, or use force against someone.
  • Do not treat suspicion, unusual appearance, job choice, or player knowledge as proof of wrongdoing.
  • Use proportionate force and follow available procedures where practical.
  • Do not abuse brig access, restraints, weapons, or security systems to harass or punish players.
  • Do not ignore clear evidence or knowingly frame an uninvolved character without a valid antagonist or roleplay basis.
  • Non-security crew should not routinely hunt suspected antagonists or act as vigilantes when security can reasonably respond.

Security is still part of the roleplay environment. Being arrested or investigated does not automatically mean another player is breaking rules; raise genuine concerns with staff rather than retaliating in-game.

10. Antagonist conduct & objectives

Antagonists are expected to create engaging conflict and pursue their assigned objectives in a way that gives the round direction. Antagonist status changes what your character may do; it does not remove the requirement to respect other players.

10.1 Follow your role and objectives

Understand the role you have been assigned and make a reasonable effort to pursue its objectives. Do not use antagonist status as an excuse for unrelated harassment, indiscriminate disruption, or actions that have no connection to your role.

10.2 Create opportunities for interaction

Where the role permits, build tension, use deception, make demands, create dilemmas, and allow other characters a chance to react. Avoid ending a scene instantly when a more interactive approach is reasonably available.

10.3 Target selection

Do not target players because of out-of-character disputes, friendships, previous rounds, or personal grudges. Choose actions based on in-character circumstances and your antagonist role.

10.4 Objectives do not excuse everything

Objectives are not blanket permission to exploit bugs, metagame, use external coordination, evade staff, or engage in out-of-character harassment. If an objective appears impossible, unclear, or conflicts with a rule, contact staff.

10.5 Round progression

Do not deliberately prolong a round after meaningful objectives and conflict have concluded merely to prevent the round from ending. Work toward a reasonable conclusion and avoid repeatedly obstructing the shuttle or round-ending process without a valid in-character purpose.

11. AI, cyborgs & silicon policy

AI and cyborg roles have broad access to station systems and can significantly affect the entire round. Players in these roles are expected to use their abilities carefully and consistently with their laws, role limitations, and the current situation.

11.1 Follow laws and directives

Follow your active laws and valid directives according to the game's law hierarchy and mechanics. Do not deliberately misread, ignore, or manipulate laws to justify behaviour that is clearly outside your role.

11.2 Do not abuse station control

Do not use cameras, doors, alarms, announcements, communications, or other system access to harass players, spy without cause, trap uninvolved crew, or prevent ordinary gameplay. Use station control in a manner consistent with your role and laws.

11.3 Law changes and antagonists

If your laws are changed or you become compromised, follow the applicable game mechanics and antagonist expectations. Do not intentionally reveal your status, sabotage your own role, or undermine your assigned side solely to avoid consequences or help friends.

11.4 When unable to continue

If you cannot reasonably continue playing an AI or cyborg role, use the available in-game procedures or contact staff for guidance. Do not abandon critical responsibilities without taking reasonable steps to hand over or resolve the situation.

Important: This section is a general policy. Specific law hierarchy, silicon exceptions, and antagonist-specific restrictions should be documented separately if Nova Station introduces custom lawsets or silicon mechanics.

12. Chat, streaming & external communication

12.1 Channel separation

Keep IC and OOC communication in their intended channels. Do not use IC channels to argue about rules or staff decisions, and do not use OOC channels to transmit information that gives living players an advantage in the current round.

12.2 Voice, streams and recordings

Streaming and recording must not be used to provide live intelligence to players in the round. Streamers should moderate chat so viewers do not reveal antagonist identities, locations, objectives, or other current-round information. Staff may ask for reasonable steps to address stream-sniping or metagaming concerns.

12.3 Language and accessibility

Communicate in a way that allows the people involved to understand important roleplay and staff instructions. Be patient with new players and people who may need clarification. Do not use language differences as an excuse to exclude or harass others.

13. Bugs, exploits & server integrity

  • Do not intentionally exploit bugs, duplication glitches, unintended access, or broken mechanics for personal advantage.
  • Do not distribute instructions for abusing an exploit in public channels or encourage others to use it.
  • Report reproducible bugs to staff or the designated development channel with enough detail to investigate.
  • Do not interfere with server services, attempt unauthorised access, attack infrastructure, or disrupt other players' connections.
  • Do not impersonate developers, claim false authority, or misrepresent a bug report or staff instruction.

Good-faith bug reporting is welcome. Accidentally encountering a bug is not the same as deliberately abusing it; stop using the unintended behaviour and report it.

14. Staff authority, investigations & enforcement

14.1 Follow staff instructions

Staff are authorised to interpret and enforce these rules, investigate reports, and take reasonable action to protect the server. Cooperate with staff requests, answer questions honestly, and do not obstruct an investigation.

14.2 Be respectful during a staff interaction

You may respectfully disagree with a decision or ask for clarification. Do not threaten, harass, spam, insult, or attempt to pressure staff into changing a decision during an active investigation. Use the appropriate appeal or complaint process afterward.

14.3 Possible disciplinary action

Depending on severity, context, intent, impact, and prior history, staff may use one or more of the following measures:

Informal correction

Guidance, clarification, or a request to stop a behaviour.

Formal warning

A documented warning that further violations may lead to stronger action.

Removal or suspension

A kick, temporary ban, role restriction, or other time-limited measure.

Permanent restriction

A permanent ban or community restriction for serious or repeated conduct.

14.4 Discretion and consistency

Not every situation can be anticipated by a written rule. Staff may act against behaviour that clearly violates the purpose of these standards, even if the exact scenario is not listed. Staff should consider relevant context and aim for proportionate, consistent outcomes.

14.5 No staff impersonation or retaliation

Do not impersonate staff, fabricate staff statements, interfere with an investigation, or retaliate against someone for making a good-faith report. Staff status does not grant permission to harass players or misuse authority.

15. Reporting concerns & appealing decisions

15.1 Reporting a player

Use the in-game report or help system, or the official Circuit Verse Community support channel. Provide a concise account of what happened, when it happened, the people involved, and any relevant evidence. Do not turn public chat into a trial or encourage others to dogpile the accused player.

15.2 Reporting a technical issue

For bugs and development issues, include steps to reproduce the problem, what you expected to happen, what actually happened, and any relevant screenshots or logs. Avoid posting private information or credentials.

15.3 Appealing a punishment

If you believe a punishment was mistaken or disproportionate, use the official appeal process. Explain your perspective calmly, provide relevant context, and acknowledge any part of the incident you could have handled differently. Repeatedly contacting individual staff members, pinging leadership, or arguing in public channels is not a substitute for an appeal.

15.4 Staff complaints

Concerns about staff conduct should be submitted through the designated complaint process and reviewed by an appropriate higher-ranking staff member or community leadership. Keep the complaint factual and provide supporting evidence where possible.

16. Common terms

IC — In Character

Actions, speech, and knowledge belonging to your character in the game world.

OOC — Out of Character

Communication between players outside the fictional world.

Metagaming

Using information your character could not reasonably know.

Powergaming

Pursuing mechanical advantage in a way that disregards fair play or roleplay.

Griefing

Deliberately disrupting the round or other players' ability to participate.

Antagonist

A role assigned special objectives and permissions by the round's game mode.

Final word: These rules are intended to support good-faith play, not to provide a checklist for avoiding consequences. Use common sense, respect the people sharing the station with you, and ask staff when you are unsure.
